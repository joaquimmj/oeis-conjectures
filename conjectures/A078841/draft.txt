%C A078841 The conjecture is true: writing m = 2^(n-j)*u with u odd and Omega(u) = j, the map m -> m/2^n is an increasing bijection from the n-almost primes onto {u/2^Omega(u): u odd, Omega(u) <= n}, whose n+1 smallest elements do not depend on n (elements with Omega(u) = j are >= (3/2)^j). Hence a(n) = 2^n*t(n+1), where t(1) <= t(2) <= ... enumerates V = {u/2^Omega(u): u odd}; since V contains p/2 for every odd prime p, the prime number theorem gives t(k+1)/t(k) -> 1, so a(n+1)/a(n) -> 2. - _Joaquim Manji-Jung_, Nov 19 2026
